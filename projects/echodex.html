BOXELS EchoDex Prototype

A playable C++ research-terminal prototype built to explore creature discovery, research progression, player profiles, researcher upgrades, regional progression, and data-driven creature systems.

ROLE Creator / Gameplay Designer / Programmer
TOOLS C++ / Visual Studio / Git / GitHub
STATUS Playable Prototype / In Development
FOCUS Creature Research & Progression Systems
01

Project Overview

BOXELS is a creature-focused game project centered around discovery, research, exploration, creature behavior, and systemic worldbuilding.

The EchoDex prototype was created as a focused gameplay experiment to test how the player could interact with creature data, progress as a researcher, unlock new regions, and gradually build knowledge about the world.

Prototype Goals

  • Create a readable creature research loop
  • Build meaningful progression around discovery
  • Organize large amounts of creature data
  • Develop a reusable UI/navigation system
  • Test player feedback through audio and visual states
02

Creature Research System

ECHODEX RESEARCH SCREENSHOT

Researching a creature is designed as a multi-step interaction instead of a single button press. The player confirms the research action, begins the research sequence, sketches the creature, receives completion feedback, and increases that creature's research level.

Reaching maximum research provides stronger feedback and prevents additional research attempts, reinforcing progression while clearly communicating the creature's completed status.

03

EchoDex & Creature Data

ECHODEX CREATURE PROFILE SCREENSHOT

Each creature contains structured information including type, habitat, rarity, temperament, diet, behavior, statistics, abilities, moves, research progression, and journal information.

The EchoDex presents that information through a consistent interface intended to make each creature feel like a research subject rather than a simple collectible entry.

04

Player Progression

Research Levels

Individual creatures progress through multiple research levels as the player continues studying them.

Researcher Upgrades

Player progression includes upgrades that improve the research experience and provide additional long-term goals.

Region Unlocking

New regions and environments become available as research progression increases.

Save Profiles

Research progress, creature discovery, and player profile information are stored across multiple save slots.

05

UI & Player Feedback

TERMINAL UI SCREENSHOT

The interface went through repeated redesigns as the prototype grew. Layout spacing, navigation, confirmation windows, warning states, sound cues, research feedback, and save/load screens were adjusted based on usability testing.

The goal was to keep the terminal aesthetic while ensuring the player could quickly understand what was selected, what action was taking place, and how to return to the previous screen.

06

Technical Implementation

Data-Driven Creature Structure

Creature information is organized into structured records so the same systems can display and process many different creatures without rebuilding the interface for each one.

Reusable Gameplay States

Navigation, confirmation dialogs, research states, progression, and save/load functionality are separated into reusable gameplay flows.

07

Iteration & Challenges

Navigation

Menu behavior was repeatedly adjusted to ensure keyboard navigation remained predictable across different screens.

Visual Hierarchy

Dense information screens required multiple layout passes to create better spacing and clearer information grouping.

Feedback Timing

Audio and animation timing during research sequences was tuned so actions felt deliberate instead of instantly jumping between states.

Scope

The prototype expanded significantly, requiring systems to become more reusable and data-driven as new features were introduced.

08

What I Learned

Building the EchoDex prototype has strengthened my understanding of how gameplay systems, interface design, progression, player feedback, data organization, and iteration all affect one another.

It has also reinforced the importance of testing systems as playable interactions instead of only designing them on paper.
